Selection of data visualization design projects from my published work. All figures designed and produced by me. Tools include Adobe Illustrator (composition, typography, layout), MATLAB, R/ggplot2, and Python/matplotlib with ImageJ plugins (data panels). Each figure was designed with deliberate choices about hierarchy, color, and visual storytelling.

**Synthesizing complex relationships of brain systems with simple schematics**

Three panels build a structural-functional picture of how the songbird brain produces and perceives song. **(a)** A side-view schematic locates the vocal motor pathway in real anatomical space; arrow shading encodes sub-network membership. **(b)** An aligned second view shows the auditory system with an inhibition/excitation waveform inset providing temporal context. **(c)** A circuit diagram renders the same anatomical regions as functional units, mapping each to a behavioral function. Restrained linework and a limited color palette let the relationships tell the story. Designed in Adobe Illustrator.

**Edwards JA**. **2025**. [Play with the changes: Innate rules for learned vocal communication in songbirds](https://academiccommons.columbia.edu/doi/10.7916/7bt6-xb79). *PhD Dissertation*. Columbia University.

**Teaching abstract math through visual sequence**

This figure walks the reader through how an NLP-style sequence model evaluates predictions on acoustic data. Three panels build the argument: matrix multiplication shown literally as a visual operation **(a)**, per-position alignment scores broken out into small multiples **(b)**, and a real-data spectrogram comparison with a final cost calculation **(c)**. The red highlighting in (a) and (b) directs the eye through the math. Designed in MATLAB and Adobe Illustrator. 

**Edwards JA**, Woolley SMN. **2026**. [A species rules syntax model accurately organizes birdsong syllables into songs](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-44602-5). *Scientific Reports* 16: 14795

**Multi-modal comparison with quantitative depth**

A side-by-side comparison across three experimental groups, designed to support causal inference about what's learned vs. inherited. Spectrograms **(a)** use color-coded ROI boxes to make corresponding song elements traceable across groups, with the same elements isolated and paired in **(b)**; the network insets distill sequence structure into one glance. Subject-group color established in (a) persists through the bar plots of **(c)** and the bias dot panel **(d)** that compresses nine quantitative metrics into a single readout of where groups differ and where they don't. Designed in MATLAB, R/ggplot2, and Adobe Illustrator. 

**Edwards JA**, Rivera M, Woolley SMN. **2025**. [The temporal organization of learned vocal behavior is predicted by species rather than experience](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/45/11/e0576242025). *Journal of Neuroscience* 45: e0576242025

<img src='/assets/images/datavis_dissertation_ephys.png'>

**Methodology, stimulus, and response in one composition**

A multi-panel figure fusing three different kinds of data into a single visual argument. **(a)** Schematic of recording electrode geometry with anatomical context. **(b)** Histological reconstruction of electrode tracks shown in two data channels: fluorescence (*left*) and Nissl stain (*right*), validating the correct positioning of the electrode. **(c)** Stimulus spectrograms with species-coded syllable boxes sit above their corresponding neural raster plots, time-aligned so the response can be read directly against the sound that produced it. The design was intentionally constructed as a vertical stack: stimulus on top, response below, with time as the shared x-axis. Designed in MATLAB, Adobe Illustrator, and ImageJ.

**Edwards JA**. **2025**. [Play with the changes: Innate rules for learned vocal communication in songbirds](https://academiccommons.columbia.edu/doi/10.7916/7bt6-xb79). *PhD Dissertation*. Columbia University.
